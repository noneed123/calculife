Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

Proof 1: How Fluid Inflow Accumulates (Integration)

When water flows into a vessel at rate \( Q(t) \) volume units per unit time, the rate of change of volume \( V(t) \) with respect to time is defined by the first derivative:

$$ \frac{dV}{dt} = Q(t) $$

Separating variables and taking the definite integral from initial time \( t = 0 \) to current time \( t \):

$$ \int_{V_0}^{V(t)} dV = \int_0^t Q(u) \, du $$ $$ V(t) - V_0 = Q \cdot t \quad (\text{for constant } Q) $$ $$ V(t) = V_0 + Q \cdot t $$

For a cylindrical or prismatic tank with cross-sectional area \( A \), the fluid height is \( h(t) = \frac{V(t)}{1000 \cdot A} \). Differentiating both sides gives the rate of height rise:

$$ \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{1}{1000 \cdot A} \cdot \frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{Q}{1000 \cdot A} $$
Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs)

Proof 2: Newton's Law of Cooling (Separation of Variables)

Newton's experimental law states that the rate of temperature loss is proportional to the difference between liquid temperature \( T(t) \) and environmental temperature \( T_{env} \):

$$ \frac{dT}{dt} = -k (T - T_{env}) $$

Separating variables:

$$ \frac{1}{T - T_{env}} \, dT = -k \, dt $$ $$ \int \frac{1}{T - T_{env}} \, dT = \int -k \, dt $$ $$ \ln |T - T_{env}| = -kt + C $$ $$ T(t) - T_{env} = e^C \cdot e^{-kt} = (T_0 - T_{env}) e^{-kt} $$ $$ T(t) = T_{env} + (T_0 - T_{env}) e^{-kt} $$

Why Calculus Matters in Daily Life

From civil engineering water distribution to culinary thermodynamics, rates of change govern our physical reality.

💧

Municipal Water & Fluid Dynamics

Reservoir engineers calculate tank fill and drainage rates daily to prevent water hammer, optimize pump electrical schedules, and manage municipal storage towers.

🌡️

Thermodynamics & Food Science

Newton's cooling law dictates how HVAC thermostats cycle air conditioning, how baristas estimate milk steaming heat retention, and how medical vaccines are cold-stored.

🚗

Kinematics & Autonomous Motion

Modern vehicle speedometers and ABS anti-lock braking systems sample instantaneous position derivatives (\( v = ds/dt \)) thousands of times per second to ensure safety.
